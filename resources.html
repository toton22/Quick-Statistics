Textbooks and references

These are the books named in your syllabus. Two of them do most of the heavy lifting on this site, so if you only open two, open those.

Topic pages cite chapter and page numbers from the two reference books (marked below). So when a page says "Anderson, Ch. 3, p. 157", you can go straight to the right spot in the library copy.

Prescribed textbooks

Textbook 1 · Cengage Learning

Statistics for Business and Economics

Anderson, D.R., Sweeney, D.J., Williams, T.A., Camm, J.D. and Cochran, J.J.

Used on this site · 12th edition

The backbone of this course. Clear business examples, a short "Notes and Comments" box after many sections, and the case problems your assignments come from (Pelican Stores, Heavenly Chocolates, Specialty Toys, Metropolitan Research, Consumer Research).

Textbook 2 · Pearson Education

Statistics for Managers Using Microsoft Excel

Levine, D.M., Szabat, K.A. and Stephan, D.F.

Used on this site · 8th edition

The Excel-first book. Each chapter ends with an "Excel Guide" showing the worksheet, function or ToolPak route for its methods. Our regression slides in class are built from it.

Textbook 3 · McGraw Hill Education, New Delhi

Business Research Methods

Cooper, D.R. and Schindler, P.S.

The research side of the course: research design, measurement, sampling plans and questionnaires. Read it for Unit 1 and for planning your capstone.

Textbook 4 · Pearson Education, New Delhi

Statistics for Management

Levin, R.I. and Rubin, D.S.

A long-time favourite in Indian business schools. Lots of solved numericals, which makes it handy for pen-and-paper exam practice.

Textbook 5 · Cengage Learning

Business Research Methods

Zikmund, W.G., Babin, B.J., Carr, J.C. and Griffin, M.

A second research-methods text. Good on survey research, measurement and scaling, so it pairs well with Unit 5's survey design topic.

Textbook 6 · Cengage Learning

Business Analytics: Data Analysis and Decision Making

Albright, S.C. and Winston, W.L.

Spreadsheet-based analytics. Useful when you want to go past the syllabus on regression, forecasting and modelling in Excel.

Supplementary references

W. W. Norton

Naked Statistics: Stripping the Dread from the Data

Wheelan, C.

A light, funny read about intuition. Read it on the bus, not the night before the exam.

Microsoft Press

Microsoft Excel Data Analysis and Business Modeling

Winston, W.L.

The how-do-I-do-this-in-Excel book. Short chapters, one technique each.

O'Reilly Media

Practical Statistics for Data Scientists

Bruce, P. and Bruce, A.

A bridge from this course to data science, if you are heading towards analytics roles.

John Wiley & Sons

Storytelling with Data

Knaflic, C.N.

How to make a chart that a busy manager actually understands. Read it before you build your capstone dashboard.

Pearson Education

Marketing Research: An Applied Orientation

Malhotra, N.K.

Questionnaire design, scaling, coding and cross-tabulation, which is most of Unit 5's survey topic.

Free open textbooks

Can't get the library copy this week? These are legally free, written by experienced teachers and statisticians, and between them cover most of the course. They use their own examples and notation, so expect small differences (for example, some use s2s^2s2 where others write σ^2\hat{\sigma}^2σ^2).

OpenIntro Statistics

4th edition · Diez, Çetinkaya-Rundel & Barr

Free PDF: it is pay-what-you-want, and you can set the price to zero. Clear writing, lots of exercises.

Best for: Inference: confidence intervals, tests, chi-square and regression explained gently.

Using AI responsibly
  • Verify every number. Re-run the key result in Excel (or by hand) before it goes in a report. If the AI and Excel disagree, Excel wins until you find out why.
  • No personal data in the chat box. Don't paste names, phone numbers, email IDs, Aadhaar or PAN numbers, salaries of named people, or any client data. Replace them with codes (C001, C002…) first. Rules like the EU's GDPR and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 exist for exactly this.
  • Cite it. In your method section, say which tool you used, what you asked it to do, and what you changed afterwards.
  • Know what it did. If you can't explain the test it ran and why, you can't defend the result in a viva. Or in a client meeting.
  • The exam is pen-and-paper. AI can help you practise. It can't sit the paper for you.

Indian context

A ready-made capstone idea: pull a monthly price index from MoSPI and a policy interest rate series from RBI DBIE for the same ten years. Then describe both, check the correlation, try a simple regression with a lag, and write up what a bank's treasury team should take from it. It touches four units in one project.

Good practice with downloaded data
  • Write down the source, the exact table name and the download date. You'll need all three in your report.
  • Keep the original file untouched on a "raw" sheet and do all cleaning on a copy, so anyone can audit your steps.
  • Read the notes and units before you analyse (₹ crore or ₹ lakh? base year 2012 or 2024?).
  • Check the licence. Most government and World Bank data is free to reuse with credit.
